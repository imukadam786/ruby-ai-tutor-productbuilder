# Wrong-answer card: writing guide

Every part of the card has one job. If two parts could be swapped without losing
anything, one of them gets rewritten.

## The card

| Part | Its only job | Shown on |
|---|---|---|
| What you put | The student's answer next to the correct one (built automatically) | Every question with a single answer |
| Why | The reason, in plain words, spoken to the student | Every question |
| Worked steps | The correct method, step by step | Calculation questions only |
| Real-life example | An everyday picture that makes the idea stick | Every question |
| Where you'll see this | Grade, term and topic (built automatically) | Every question |

There is no "How to fix it" text. On calculation questions the worked steps fill
that slot; on fact questions it is left out.

## Why (the question's memo)

- Speak to the student ("you"), never "the learner".
- At most 2 sentences and about 40 words. No sentence over 25 words.
- Read at or below the learner's grade. Explain any hard word inside the sentence.
- Keep every fact from the original. Add no new facts.
- Never mention option codes (e1, p2, x3) or "items". Students never see them;
  name the option by what it says.
- On a calculation question: talk about the mistake, never do the calculation.

## Worked steps (calculation questions)

- Numbered, one step per line, one operation per step.
- Numbers and working only. Don't explain the mistake (that's the Why's job).
- The last step lands on the answer.

## Real-life example

- An everyday South African picture: taxis, spaza shops, soccer, school, family,
  airtime and data, braais, payslips.
- 1 to 2 sentences, at most about 40 words.
- No facts, names, dates, terms or numbers from the question or topic.
- No instructions ("Remember to…", "Test the claim…"). That's not its job.
- No method or working. On a calculation question it must not repeat the steps.
- The picture has to match the idea exactly, not just loosely.
- Sensitive topics (slavery, genocide, apartheid violence): compare the
  structure (who made the rules, who was left out), never the suffering, and
  never with a game or a joke.

## Everywhere

- No em dashes. Use a full stop, comma or colon.
- South African spelling and the decimal comma (2,5 not 2.5).
