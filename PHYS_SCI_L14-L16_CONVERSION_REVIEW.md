## L14.T1.A2 — pH, pOH and Kw

Q: Define pH.  
- ✗ pH = +log₁₀[H⁺].  
- ✅ pH = −log₁₀[H⁺] (the negative log of the hydrogen-ion concentration).  
- ✗ pH = −log₁₀[OH⁻].  

**L14.T1.A2.q04** · ERR_POH_PH_RELATION  
Q: State the relationship between pH and pOH at 25°C.  
- ✗ pH + pOH = 7.  
- ✗ pH × pOH = 14.  
- ✅ pH + pOH = 14 (at 25 °C).  

**L14.T1.A2.q06** · ERR_KW_DEFINITION  
Q: What is Kw at 25°C, and what is its physical significance?  
- ✅ Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ — the ion product of water.  
- ✗ Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻⁷.  
- ✗ Kw = 14.  

**L14.T1.A2.q11** · ERR_NEUTRAL_PH_TEMPERATURE

- ✗ Yes — pH 7 is neutral at any temperature.  
- ✅ Only at 25 °C — Kw changes with temperature, so neutral pH differs at other temperatures.  
- ✗ No — pH 7 is always acidic.  

**L14.T1.A2.q16** · ERR_DILUTION_PH

- ✗ Both [H⁺] and pH stay the same.

## L15.T1.A1 — Collision theory & rate factors

**L15.T1.A1.q01** · ERR_RATE_DEFINITION  
Q: Define REACTION RATE.  
- ✗ The total amount of product formed in a reaction.  
- ✅ The change in concentration of a reactant or product per unit time.  
- ✗ The time taken to reach equilibrium.  

**L15.T1.A1.q02** · ERR_COLLISION_THEORY_INCOMPLETE  
Q: State COLLISION THEORY of reaction rates.  
- ✗ Particles react whenever they collide, regardless of energy or orientation.  
- ✗ Reactions occur when particles are heated, without any collisions.  
- ✅ Particles must collide with sufficient energy (≥ Ea) AND the correct orientation.  

**L15.T1.A1.q03** · ERR_RATE_CONCENTRATION, ERR_COLLISION_FREQUENCY  
Q: How does INCREASING THE CONCENTRATION of reactants affect the reaction rate? Explain using collision theory.  
- ✅ Increases the rate — more particles per volume means more frequent collisions.  
- ✗ Decreases the rate — crowded particles collide less.  
- ✗ No effect — concentration doesn't influence the rate.  

**L15.T1.A1.q04** · ERR_RATE_SURFACE_AREA  
Q: How does INCREASING the SURFACE AREA of a solid reactant affect the reaction rate?  
- ✗ Decreases the rate — more surface slows contact.  
- ✅ Increases the rate — more exposed particles means more frequent collisions.  
- ✗ No effect — surface area is irrelevant to rate.  

**L15.T1.A1.q05** · ERR_RATE_TEMPERATURE, ERR_T_VS_EA  
Q: How does INCREASING the TEMPERATURE affect the reaction rate? Explain.  
- ✗ Decreases the rate — heat disperses the particles.  
- ✗ Increases the rate only by raising the activation energy.  
- ✅ Increases the rate — particles have more energy and a larger fraction exceed Ea.  

**L15.T1.A1.q06** · ERR_CATALYST_ROLE, ERR_CATALYST_EA  
Q: What is the role of a CATALYST in a reaction? How does it affect the rate?  
- ✅ It provides an alternative pathway with lower activation energy, increasing the rate.  
- ✗ It increases the activation energy, slowing the reaction.  
- ✗ It increases the kinetic energy of the particles.  

**L15.T1.A1.q07** · ERR_RATE_PRESSURE  
Q: How does INCREASING the PRESSURE of a GASEOUS reaction affect the rate?  
- ✗ Decreases the rate — higher pressure separates gas particles.  
- ✅ Increases the rate — compressing the gas raises the concentration, so more collisions.  
- ✗ No effect on gaseous reaction rates.  

**L15.T1.A1.q08** · ERR_ACTIVATION_ENERGY  
Q: What is ACTIVATION ENERGY (Ea)?  
- ✗ The energy released when products form.  
- ✗ The average kinetic energy of all the particles.  
- ✅ The minimum kinetic energy colliding particles need for an effective (successful) collision.  

**L15.T1.A1.q09** · ERR_CATALYST_EA, ERR_CATALYST_KE  
Q: A learner says: 'A catalyst increases the kinetic energy of the reactant particles.' Is this correct?
